I'm a PhD candidate in Statistics at [Bowling Green State University](https://www.bgsu.edu/), advised by Prof. Wei Ning. I develop statistical methods that stay reliable when standard assumptions fail.

My dissertation introduces **Approximate Empirical Likelihood BART (AEL-BART)**, a robust alternative to Bayesian additive regression trees. AEL-BART localizes empirical likelihood within the nodes of each tree. In simulations with heavy-tailed errors, it substantially reduces prediction error relative to standard BART. I also work on sensitivity analysis for unmeasured confounding and on how large language models can support, or mislead, students learning statistics.

I care a great deal about teaching. I have been instructor of record for Introduction to Statistics, Fundamentals of Statistics, and Calculus at BGSU, and in 2026 I received BGSU's **Graduate Excellence in Teaching Award**. Before graduate school in statistics, I studied actuarial science in Ghana, so I like helping students see how many directions a quantitative degree can take them.
